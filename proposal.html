Abstract

Visual inspection of aero-engine blades must decide whether a change seen in an image is physical damage, and if so where it is on the part and how deep it goes. Current methods either flag anomalies in 2D, output 3D boxes or voxels, or require a measured 3D scan. None reports whether the available views are sufficient to support a claimed shape change. We propose a task and benchmark for CAD-conditioned sparse-view 3D damage estimation with explicit evidence bounds: given a healthy blade mesh and a few calibrated inspection images, output a damaged mesh where the evidence supports one, and abstain where camera error, lighting, or surface colour could explain the images equally well.

Preliminary controlled experiments on public Rotor37 blade geometry show the problem is real and measurable. A render-and-compare fit produced false dents on 14 of 14 healthy or colour-only blades under a 1° camera error. On unfamiliar dents, the best image match chose a worse 3D shape in 8 of 12 depth sweeps. An image-guided proposal method we developed then recovered 12 of 12 fresh dent locations exactly and kept 18 of 18 dents exact, with 0 of 14 false dents, under the same 1° error. The proposal defines what must be added before submission: continuous or learned estimation, sealed test geometries, strong baselines, and at least one real measured capture.

1Problem and motivation

Blade damage such as dents, nicks, scratches and corrosion is often a few pixels across in inspection imagery. Our first project phase showed that an open vision-language model (Qwen2-VL-7B) named the defect correctly in only 2% of dented and 5% of nicked synthetic blades. A multi-agent checker grounded in a YOLOv8 detector raised this to 100% and 99% and caught 96–98% of planted report errors, including on 125 held-out images. But that work operates on independent 2D images: it can say "a dent near the centre of the frame", not "a 0.1 mm-deep dent at this point on the pressure side".

Maintenance decisions depend on the physical change. A dark patch in a photo can be a dent, a stain, a reflection, a lighting change, or a misregistered camera. Inspection AI should therefore answer two questions: what physical change best explains these images, and do the images contain enough evidence to distinguish it from non-damage explanations.

The closest work is CADSplat, which fits a shared deformation field from sparse posed RGB and a CAD prior, and explicitly leaves measurement-quality deformed surfaces for future work. Looking 3D compares a photo with reference CAD but outputs boxes. PASDF repairs shape but needs a point cloud. Commercial tools (Waygate Real3D, Evident 3DAssist, GE Aerospace's blade inspection tool) already measure defects with dedicated 3D hardware, so the contribution must be scientific, not a product claim.

3Research gaps

  1. Physical change from sparse RGB + CAD. No surveyed method outputs a validated damaged surface from a few images and a healthy design.
  2. Damage versus look-alikes. No method states whether the views can distinguish a physical change from camera, lighting, appearance or reference mismatch. Our data shows this failure directly (Section 6).
  3. No paired benchmark. No public set combines blade images, healthy CAD, exact damaged meshes, healthy and colour-only negatives, and controlled capture perturbations.
  4. Synthetic-to-real collapse. Our synthetic-trained detector fell from 0.986 to 0.606 image AUROC and from 0.744 to 0.040 mask IoU on real AeBAD-S photographs.
  5. No real 3D validation data. No public dataset pairs real blade photographs with measured damage geometry.

4Proposed contribution

We propose three linked contributions. Each has a test that would falsify it.

ContributionWhat is newFalsified if
C1 · Task and benchmarkSparse-view, CAD-conditioned 3D damage estimation with three outputs: damaged mesh, location, and "supported / not supported". Rotor37-based cases with dents, colour-only changes, mixtures, healthy controls and capture perturbations; held-out blade shapes sealed until the method is frozen.Simple shortcut controls (text, colour statistics, image-difference magnitude alone) solve it.
C2 · Evidence-bounded estimatorImage-guided location proposals, continuous render-and-compare refinement of a local displacement field, a separate appearance variable, and an evidence test that compares the best "no damage" explanation against the best damage explanation, calibrated on healthy captures.It does not beat a plain fit, a pose ensemble, and a CADSplat-style deformation fit at matched false-deformation rate.
C3 · FindingsQuantified failure modes: image agreement versus 3D accuracy, false dents from sub-degree camera error, and the gap between locating damage and recovering its depth.The effects vanish on sealed shapes or on the real capture.

The evidence test is the core idea. With d the local deformation and η the nuisance variables (camera, illumination, surface appearance, reference mismatch):

fit_gain = minη L(d = 0, η) − mind, η L(d, η)

A damage claim is reported only when the fit gain exceeds a threshold set on healthy and colour-only calibration captures. Otherwise the output is "not supported by these views". Hypothesis testing, inverse rendering and uncertainty are each established tools; the claim is only new if this combination demonstrably lowers false physical-change claims at equal damage recall, against strong matched baselines.

5Data and evaluation plan

Data

  • Development: public PLAID Rotor37 shapes 9–12 (CC BY-SA 4.0), already exposed. Synthetic dents, colour patches and mixtures rendered in Blender at known cameras and lighting.
  • Sealed test: at least 6 further Rotor37 shapes opened only once the method is frozen, with fresh damage locations, depths between candidate values, and new capture perturbations.
  • Real validation (required): a 3D-printed Rotor37 blade with a controlled indentation; calibrated photos (ChArUco board); a structured-light or photogrammetry scan as ground truth. Even 3–5 measured cases would close the most serious reviewer objection.
  • Context: BladeSynth, AeBAD and AEBIS results reported for the 2D synthetic-to-real gap.

Metrics

  • Local and whole-blade 3D RMS error versus unchanged CAD; damage-centre surface distance; depth error.
  • False physical-change rate on healthy and colour-only negatives; risk–coverage curves for the "supported" decision.
  • Grouped 95% bootstrap intervals by blade shape; paired tests; every case retained, including failures.

Baselines and ablations

  • Unchanged CAD; shape-only fit; fixed normal-projection rule; our earlier learned shape-change model.
  • CADSplat-style deformation fitting and CAD-NeRF where code is available; 2D detector (AEBIS model) lifted onto the mesh.
  • Ablations: no appearance variable, no image-guided proposals, no evidence test, camera jitter on/off, two vs. more views.

7Conference strategy

Why this venue. The closest prior work (Looking 3D, PIAD, Bayes' Rays, ModMap) appears at the same top vision venue, and the industrial anomaly and 3D reconstruction communities review there. The paper fits the "3D from multi-view and sensors" and "vision applications" areas.

Submission gates on 1 November. We submit to the main track only if all of these hold:

A rushed main-track submission that reviewers reject on "synthetic only, nonlearned" grounds costs more than a strong later submission.
