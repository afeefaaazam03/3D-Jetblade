1 · The core problem

One dark spot, four possible causes

Close-ups from the test photos. Lighting changes and a camera moved by about 1° also change the image, without any damage.

In a single photo, all four can look almost the same. Our method uses the CAD model and a few calibrated photos to tell them apart, or abstains when the photos are not enough.

2 · How it works: render and compare
Explain without damage: fit camera, lighting and stains.
Explain with damage: add a dent, nick or crack and fit its 3D shape.
Decide: report only if it fits clearly better (threshold set on healthy and stained blades), otherwise abstain.

⑤ Damage type: specialists + judge (the MedGuards idea)

MedGuards checks medical text with several specialist agents and a judge that settles disagreements by confidence. We do the same with physical models: each specialist tries to explain the photos in its own way, at the spot found in ④.

“Clear” uses the same evidence scale as ④: the winner must beat the runner-up by at least the detection threshold. The judge only labels damage that ④ already found, so it cannot add false alarms.

5 · Identifiability finding

Why depth is the hard part

A real example on an industrial blade: the dent was found in the right place, but measured 10× too shallow. In two ordinary photos, a small, sharp 0.06 mm dent and a 0.6 mm dent can produce nearly identical pixels.

Our experiments show depth is not reliably identifiable from two photos: on new designs found dents are measured at a median 60% of their true depth. Better search, more starting points and four cameras instead of two did not fix it, and depth ranges contained the true value only 36 of 109 times.

So: where the damage is, is reliable; how deep needs more information, most likely low-angle (grazing) lighting. This is the next experiment.

6 · 3D localisation
0.7 mm
median 3D centre error

We know where the damage is on the blade: on the sealed Rotor37 test, located dents are found a median 0.7 mm (mean 0.8 mm) from their true centre, on blades about 74 mm long.

Other test sets: unseen industrial designs mean 0.6 mm; realistic nicks and cracks mean 1.9 mm (long cracks make “centre” less precise).

Expert feedback

What researchers in the field told us

We contacted authors of the closest published work. Their advice shaped the framing, the baselines and the next experiments.

Author of Looking 3D (CVPR 2024)
Reference-based anomaly detection
PAD citedVGGT not yet
Looking 3D is not precise enough for this problem. Try VGGT with Sim(3) alignment, or PAD if many images are available; training a new model would need very large GPU resources.
Impact on this project

Confirmed that reconstruction-from-scratch methods differ from our CAD-prior approach. PAD is cited in our related work; VGGT + Sim(3) alignment has not been tried yet.

Author of CADSplat (2026)
CAD-prior sparse-view reconstruction
Gap supportedDeflectometry: future
CADSplat cannot detect minor defects; its deformation network is noisy, and they are moving to signed distance fields. Suggested phase-measuring deflectometry for small defects.
Impact on this project

Supports our research gap from the closest method. Our paper cites only CADSplat’s public statement that measurement-quality surfaces are future work. Deflectometry (structured light on shiny surfaces) fits our planned lighting experiment.

Second reply (anomaly detection)
Practical baseline advice
2D baselines donePatchCore: to do
Start with a simple memory-bank detector (PatchCore) to find defects, then compute the depth difference against a nominal anchor area.
Impact on this project

We implemented a 2D U-Net detector and a SIFT change detector as baselines; our method locates more than twice as many dents. PatchCore and the nominal-anchor depth comparison are not yet run.

Author of PIAD (CVPR 2025)
Pose- and illumination-agnostic anomaly detection
Reframed
The problem is fundamentally difficult. Making a model invariant to lighting removes depth cues. Studying identifiability, and abstaining when the evidence is insufficient, is a meaningful contribution in itself.
Impact on this project

The turning point: we reframed the work around identifiability and abstention rather than depth accuracy alone. Our results agree: location is reliable, depth needs better illumination.

Synthetic-data expert
Synthetic data for vision
Lighting + camera doneNoise/blur: to do
Synthetic data needs variability and realism (noise, blur, lighting, camera angles). Quantity matters, but realism is key: aim for a pipeline close to the real world.
Impact on this project

Partly matches our benchmark: photorealistic Blender renders with lighting changes and camera errors, about 1,350 cases. Not yet modelled: sensor noise, motion blur and material variety. These are the next realism step.
