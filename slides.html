The problem: damage is tiny, and AI reports about it are confidently wrong

Blade inspectors look at camera images and decide: is there damage, where is it, how bad is it?

And a dark patch in a photo can be a dent, a stain, a lighting change or a 1° camera error.

Right: real BladeSynth image, 46 pixels of corrosion. The VLM said "Defect: None, PASS". Our Phase 1 checker fixed reports using a detector (Dent 2% to 100%, Nick 5% to 99%), but it can only say "a dent somewhere in the photo", not where on the part or how deep.

The research question

Given the healthy design and a few calibrated photos, which physical damage do the images actually support?

Input

Healthy CAD mesh + 2 photos, nominal camera and lights

→

Output 1

Damaged 3D shape: where, how deep, how wide

Output 2 · the new part

"Supported" or "not supported by these photos", with a controlled false-alarm rate

The key word is "supported". Existing methods give anomaly heatmaps, boxes or voxels, or need a 3D scanner. None says whether the views are enough to distinguish a real dent from a stain, a light change or a slightly wrong camera. That decision, with a calibrated false-alarm rate, is our contribution.

Method: explain the photos without damage first, then ask if a dent does clearly better

1 · No-damage fit

Camera from the outline, then lighting and any stain

2 · Score every spot

One gradient pass rates a small dent or stain anywhere

3 · Continuous dent

Position, depth, size, shape and rim, same nuisance freedom

4 · Decision

Report only if the fit gain beats a threshold set on calibration blades

Built on a differentiable renderer with a light-linear neural shader (0.9 grey-level error on an unseen blade), and judged only on reliable interior pixels.

Fit gain = loss without dent minus loss with dent; the dent model contains "no dent", so the gain is never negative. The threshold is a split-conformal quantile on healthy and stain-only calibration blades, which bounds the false-change rate. Step 2 is a Rao score test: one backward pass gives the evidence for a small dent at every surface location and radius.
